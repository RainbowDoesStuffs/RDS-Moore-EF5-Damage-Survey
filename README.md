So at the minimum, this has been a project for two years, probably closer to four, with seeds being planted into it's development likely as early as 2022 - 2021 as I got more in-depth with the idea of damage surveying and enjoying the thought of that.

There was, admittedly, a lot wrong with some of my original takes and polygons and tornado centerlines and vortex sizes, and admittedly that's probably gonna be true even still because also, admittedly, I am not perfect nor ever will be. This version has refined the tornado's polygons and centerlines to the best absolute I could have done reasonably as one single person, this is better off in the hands of a entire scientific team as apposed to one random teenage girl.

This tornado's survey's original goal was to be as objective as possible, and that idea has stayed persistent but admittedly as I fleshed it out more thoroughly it has become a lot more subjective, but I don't think that's necessarily a bad thing as long as my primary objective also was persistent on the idea of being as objective as I can by relying on all the surrounding context and structural damage, tornado surveying is honestly a subjective science down to even the most objective scales we have, since those are still to be subjectively interpreted and mostly off precedent of what the professionals said structures of a certain type MIGHT fail at. This tornado has been a significant part of my learning on tornado dynamics and knowledge. Generally this tornado was a pretty big learning experience.

You can see that one of my decisions was to give it a (vaguely) higher end EF4 polygon map for the exceptionally violent winds, a lot of the reason behind this was to highlight the still incredibly violent winds that were inside the circulation but were not necessarily certainly ef5 winds, although I will say at some points in the EF4+ polygons I do believe it is most likely EF5 intensity, but there is no objective evidence for that or 100% certainty ; thus no EF5 polygon.

A primary take-away from this (and it's not just this tornado, it's many others.) is that tornado contextuals can be very extreme and almost seem absurdly far into the EF5 corridor (a lot of the damage is what people would describe as 300+ mph wind damage) despite the fact that the actual true windspeeds are below 201 mph by a significant amount in some areas, some of the "visually EF5" areas are actually as low as EF3 intensity, which I find to be a very interesting case study. 

my personal opinion as to why it appears like this and why the contextuals seem so extreme and exaggerated despite the (relatively) lower windspeeds? The tornado's general dwell time, a lot of time the dwell far exceeded the mark of 10 - 20 seconds, which I believe greatly exaggerated the contextual gaze of the tornado, that and how absolutely muddy it was, this tornado has " ground scouring " in areas as low as the EF1-0 corridor, which leads to an almost obvious conclusion by the immediate browning and the tornado was consistently coated in a layer of dust that the tornado was likely centrifuging and blowing extreme layers of dirt and mud all over through it's circulation, some of that was likely aided in town by the debris gouging into the ground and splattering dirt everywhere, outside? it's probably mostly tornado winds and water causing this, I don't actually believe Moore scoured grass that much but rather flattened it under layers of mud, caking it so extremely that it almost appears that it just doesn't exist anymore. A lot of tornado contextuals are actually just based on subjective perception and can be easily confused by damage just "looking" worse, which doesn't necessarily have to actually be WORSE to LOOK worse.
